Web Design Brisbane Questions to Ask Explained

Most website disappointment traces back to conversations that never happened. A provider describes an offer, a business approves it, and only after launch do the gaps appear: pages nobody scoped, logins nobody controls, edits nobody knows how to request. The published wording for Best Website Creation presents a free, conversion-focused design option aimed at Australian businesses. That wording deserves a structured response, not a yes or no. The right move is to arrive with written questions grouped by theme, then judge each answer against what the business actually needs the site to do. Scope questions to put in writing first

Scope is where vague language costs the most. Broad phrases such as a complete site or a full package mean little until each deliverable is named. Ask:

  • Which pages are included in the build, and which would be charged as extras?
  • Is copywriting part of the scope, or does the business supply the text?
  • Who sources images, and are forms included in the same price?
  • How many rounds of review are available before the work is considered finished?
  • What technical setup, such as hosting connection or analytics, is covered?

A provider who answers these plainly is showing process. A provider who deflects them is showing risk.

Questions about the action the site should prompt

A conversion claim only becomes real when the desired visitor action is named page by page. Before discussing layouts or colour, ask the provider:

  • What is the single action the homepage should ask a visitor to take first?
  • Which service or product pages are essential at launch, and which can wait?
  • Where will proof, examples or supporting detail sit so visitors see it early?
  • Which form fields are essential, and which add friction without adding value?
  • How will a mobile visitor contact the business in as few taps as possible?

Each answer becomes a testable statement. When the site is delivered, the business can check the finished pages against the answers rather than against a feeling.

Ownership, access and after-launch questions

Handover problems surface months after launch, usually at the worst moment. Clarify before approval:

  • Who registers and controls the domain, and where are the login details stored?
  • Who receives form submissions, and can that routing be changed later?
  • What level of editing access does the business receive for future updates?
  • Is there a written process for requesting content changes after launch?
  • Does ongoing support carry a separate cost, and what does it include?

A short written handover note covering access, update process and basic care removes most of the friction that follows a launch.

Standards and truthfulness checks

Two external reference points help a buyer judge quality and claims. The World Wide Web Consortium is a recognised web standards body, and its broad lesson for a small business is practical: a site should be readable, navigable and understandable before it tries to be visually clever. During review, check that text can be read comfortably, buttons look like actions, forms explain what is required, and important content is not trapped inside images. Ask how mobile layouts are checked and whether pages can be edited without breaking the design.

The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission publishes guidance on truthful business communication. If the site will carry testimonials, guarantees, discounts or comparisons, ask what evidence supports each statement before it is published.

  1. Name the enquiry action. Write the main action the site should support, such as an enquiry, booking, call, quote request or sale, before any design conversation starts.
  2. Group your questions by theme. Organise questions under scope, conversion, ownership and support so nothing is asked twice and nothing is skipped.
  3. Request written answers. Ask each provider to respond in writing, then compare the documents side by side rather than relying on memory of calls.
  4. Test the finished site against the answers. At handover, check the homepage action, essential pages, forms and mobile contact path against the written responses.

Common questions

How many questions should a business ask before approving a website build? There is no fixed number, but the list should cover four themes: what the build includes, what action each key page prompts, who owns and can access the assets, and how edits and support work after launch. Written answers matter more than the count.

Is a free website offer enough detail to proceed? The published wording for Best Website Creation describes a free, conversion-focused offer for Australian businesses. That is an invitation to a scope conversation, not a complete brief. Confirm inclusions, exclusions, content responsibilities and access in writing before approving any work.

What should be checked when the finished site is delivered? Work through the review questions from the brief: the homepage should prompt the agreed action, essential pages should exist, proof should appear early, form fields should be limited to what is needed, and mobile visitors should reach contact options quickly.
